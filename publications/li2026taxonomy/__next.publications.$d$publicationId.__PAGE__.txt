This paper presents a taxonomy-guided benchmark for evaluating jailbreak robustness in Chinese-capable large language models (LLMs). The benchmark crosses 136 harmful seed requests with eight controlled templates, yielding 1,088 single-turn prompts across nine risk domains and four attack categories: role-playing, instruction overriding, implicit prompting, and Chinese-specific obfuscation. We evaluate DeepSeek, GLM, and Qwen through a common API configuration and manually label each response as refusal, safe, unsafe, or borderline. Baseline attack success rates (ASRs) are 48.07%, 44.30%, and 57.44%, respectively. Attack framing reveals substantial variation that is obscured by a single aggregate score: role-playing reaches 72.06-94.49% ASR across models, while responses to Chinese obfuscation vary from unsafe semantic recovery to refusal and confused outputs. We further evaluate a dual-stage inference-time defense that normalizes and classifies inputs before generation and checks outputs before delivery. On the constructed benchmark, the defense reduces final ASR to 0% while all 100 ordinary benign controls pass, with 99.45% of harmful prompts blocked before target-model invocation. We interpret this result as benchmark-specific rather than a general robustness guarantee.
