e:T59a,Graph-based retrieval-augmented generation (GraphRAG) answers multi-hop questions by organizing a corpus into a graph and retrieving along its structure. Existing retrieval methods face two limitations. Diffusion- and similarity-based methods expand retrieval along fixed edge weights or by similarity to the question, without resolving its implicit entity references, allowing relevance errors to accumulate across hops. In contrast, exploration-based methods let an LLM reason about the next hop, but require a separate generation at each hop, leading to high inference costs. We observe that an LLM can resolve implicit entity references from retrieved passages and a multi-hop question before generating any tokens. Further analysis identifies a small set of attention heads, termed addressing heads, that capture this capability across multiple hops in a single forward pass. Building on this finding, we propose HyperAddress, which runs Personalized PageRank on a description-labelled hypergraph, using addressing heads to determine the restart distribution and transition weights, thereby guiding multi-hop retrieval without any generation at query time. On MuSiQue, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and HotpotQA, HyperAddress outperforms the strongest baseline by 3.5 points of Recall@5 and 7.4 points of All@5 on average, with gains that grow with the number of hops.
